\begin{abstract}

    \setstretch{1.0} 
    \noindent Whole-body loco-manipulation for humanoid robots presents significant challenges due to the high-dimensional state space and the multi-modal nature of valid solutions. This work proposes a hierarchical framework that leverages a conditional diffusion model as a high-level kinematic planner, trained on retargeted human motion capture data. By utilizing a 1D Diffusion Transformer architecture and an egocentric state representation, the model learns to synthesize diverse, locally consistent motion primitives. These primitives can be "stitched" together to generate long-horizon trajectories for novel goals not seen during training. We evaluate the framework's performance on trajectory reconstruction and generalization to out-of-distribution targets, demonstrating its effectiveness as a generative prior. Finally, we analyze current limitations regarding physical feasibility and goal reaching, laying the groundwork for future integration with physics-aware tracking controllers.
