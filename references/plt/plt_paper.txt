Physically simulated characters can learn highly natural full-body motion
guided by motion capture datasets. However, the range of motion is limited
to the existing high-quality datasets, and cannot effectively adapt to challeng-
ing scenarios. We propose a novel policy architecture that learns part-wise
motion skills, where individual parts can be separately extended and com-
bined for unobserved settings. Our method employs a set of part-specific
codebooks, which robustly capture motion dynamics without catastrophic
collapse or forgetting. This structured decomposition allows intuitive control
over the character’s behavior and dynamic exploration for a novel combi-
nation of part-wise motion. We further incorporate a refinement network
compensating for subtle discrepancies in the disjoint discrete tokens, thus
improving motion quality and stability. Our extensive evaluations show that
our part-wise latent token achieves superior performance in imitating mo-
tions, even those from unseen distribution. We also validate our method in
challenging tasks, including body tracking, navigation on complex terrains,

1 Introduction

Physically-based animation has significantly improved the realism
of simulated character motions by replicating human motions in mo-
tion capture datasets [Hassan et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2024a; Zhang
et al. 2023]. Hierarchical control pipelines learn general motion
priors from a large collection of motion data and exploit the latent
priors to obtain task-specific policy [Bohez et al. 2022; Merel et al.
2020]. Despite their success, these pipelines often struggle with
discovering novel motion skills and task adaptability. The available
high-quality datasets [Fussell et al. 2021; Peng et al. 2018, 2021] can-
not extensively encompass the wide range of daily human motions.

We argue that the aforementioned challenge in the generaliza-
tion stems from the rigidity of the motion priors, which can only
consume the provided set of whole-body motions. Some studies
have proposed combining part-wise motions from different sources
to improve data efficiency and expand the diversity of generated
motions [Bae et al. 2023; Khoshsiyar et al. 2024; Xu et al. 2023a].
However, the part-specific priors are either ignorant of compatibility
between part-level motions or not reusable for further scenarios.

To enrich the adaptability of motion prior, we propose a novel
policy architecture that learns part-wise motion skills using dis-
crete latent tokens. Part-specific codebooks contain discrete latent
tokens representing specialized motion skills for designated body
parts, constituting structured motion decomposition. The policy
architecture trains a high-level policy that combines tokens from
multiple body parts. The rich combinations of part-wise latent to-
kens increase the diversity of generated motions and significantly
improve generalization compared to whole-body motion represen-
tation with the same number of tokens. Prior work on continuous
latent spaces [Luo et al. 2023b; Peng et al. 2022; Won et al. 2022]
could be extended to decompose part-wise motion priors. How-
ever, this requires multiple high-level policies, increasing training
complexity, or a single unified policy, which loses the benefits of
decomposition. In contrast, our method allows stable yet efficient
training using a single high-level policy with a multi-discrete action
space. By dynamically combining tokens from multiple body parts,
the agent discovers optimal movements tailored to task goals.

We introduce a refinement network within the policy architec-
ture to enhance the fidelity of generated motions. While part-wise
codebooks provide structured motion decomposition, naive com-
binations of discrete embeddings can result in jerky movements.
Previous methods combining part-wise motions [Bae et al. 2023; Xu
et al. 2023a] often rely solely on physical plausibility from simula-
tion engines, limiting their applicability to complex scenarios. Our
refinement network adds continuous offsets to discrete latent to-
kens, effectively ensuring coherence across body parts. Empirically,
combining discrete tokens with continuous refinement improves
stability and adaptability across diverse scenarios.

Through experiments, we demonstrate that our part-wise method
dramatically enhances motion fidelity, task adaptability, and scala-
bility. First, our approach achieves state-of-the-art results on full-
body imitation. The imitation performance is stable even for out-of-
distribution targets, indicating the robustness and expressiveness of
part-wise latent tokens in capturing diverse motion skills. Next, we
validate our approach in task scenarios without extensive reward
engineering. In N-body tracking, our model consistently achieves
high accuracy across varying tracker configurations, even without
full-body imitation rewards. For navigation tasks, including point-
goal scenarios with damaged body parts, our method demonstrates
superior performance, showcasing its adaptability to challenging
scenarios. We further demonstrate the feasibility of our proposed
method on an extremely large-scale dataset [Mahmood et al. 2019],
addressing conventional tasks [Luo et al. 2023b] such as striking
and navigating rough terrain. Finally, we introduce part-wise mo-
tion adaptation, a novel scheme for incrementally updating motion
priors by integrating data from multiple datasets while maintain-
ing physical plausibility. For example, an agent can adapt its lower

for handball motion. Such adaptability liberates the physics-based
animation from the predefined full-body motion dataset, providing
a systematic approach to broaden the scope of generated motions.

2 Related Works
2.1 Kinematics-Based Motion Generation

Large-scale motion capture datasets [Harvey et al. 2020; Mahmood
et al. 2019; Mason et al. 2022] have incurred massive advancements
in kinematics-based motion generation. While recent generative
models can synthesize high-quality motions [Chen et al. 2023; Guo
et al. 2024; Tevet et al. 2023], these approaches only account for
skeletal movements and lack physical plausibility, resulting in ap-
parent artifacts such as foot sliding or penetration to the scene
objects.

2.2 Physics-Based Hierarchical Control Pipelines

Hierarchical control pipelines efficiently use offline dataset while
training a simulated character to move such that it performs a range
of tasks [Bohez et al. 2022; Merel et al. 2018, 2020]. Most existing
pipelines rely on latent models to represent dynamics, which can
be broadly categorized into continuous and discrete models.
Continuous latent models [Ling et al. 2020; Luo et al. 2023b;
Peng et al. 2022; Won et al. 2022; Yao et al. 2022] encode motions
in a smooth latent space with regularization like variational in-
ference [Kingma 2013]. However, these models are susceptible to
hyperparameter tuning, which often results in unstable training
and suboptimal task learning. On the other hand, discrete latent
models [Han et al. 2024; Yao et al. 2024; Zhu et al. 2023] offer
greater robustness by representing motion priors as tokenized latent
spaces [Van Den Oord et al. 2017]. While discrete models mitigate
some challenges of continuous representations, their models are
highly dependent on the number of tokens and suffer from arti-
facts like abrupt motions. MoConVQ [Yao et al. 2024] presents an
advanced discrete latent model that integrates a model-based ap-
proach with differentiable simulation layers and Residual Vector
Quantization (RVQ) [Lee et al. 2022; Zeghidour et al. 2021]. In con-
trast, our method fundamentally differs as it eliminates the need to
train simulation layers. Both continuous and discrete latent models
remain heavily dependent on the training dataset, limiting their
adaptability to novel or unseen motions. To address this, we pro-
pose a method that effectively decomposes part-wise motion priors
that significantly enhances generalization to the novel scenarios.

2.3. Part-Wise Motion Combination

Physically-based animation often requires motion capture data to
train an agent for reasonable training efficiency and motion fidelity.
To further expand the diversity of generated motions, several works
demonstrated combining part-wise motion skills [Bae et al. 2023;
Xu et al. 2023a]. These approaches lack hierarchical structures of
motion priors and can only perform the original task for which they
are trained. Another stream of works enforces low-level physical
constraints, such as contact constraints [Khoshsiyar et al. 2024],
but it is not generalizable to the other scenarios. In contrast, our
method introduces a hierarchical framework for part-wise motion

3 Background: Hierarchical Control with Discrete Prior

Hierarchical control pipelines provide a practical framework for
learning and reusing motion skills from large datasets [Bohez et al.
2022; Merel et al. 2020]. The pipeline is composed of two phases.
First, the imitation-learning phase acquires a latent space of motion
priors by imitating a large collection of motion data. Second, the
task-learning phase trains a task-specific reinforcement learning
framework exploiting the hierarchical structure. The high-level
policy from the task-learning phase samples a sequence of latent
vectors, which are decoded into a sequence of actions with low-level
policy using the decoder trained from the imitation-learning phase.

The use of a discrete action space in hierarchical control can bene-
fit reinforcement learning. The latent motion embedding using a con-
tinuous vector space is subject to posterior collapse, making down-
stream tasks less effective, as addressed in a prior work [Peng et al.
2022]. While models like PULSE [Luo et al. 2023b] mitigate this issue
by carefully tuning hyperparameters, such as KL-divergence sched-
uling and exploration rates, these adjustments are time-consuming
and impractical for large-scale training. In contrast, discrete latent
vectors, commonly used in image generation [Esser et al. 2021;
Razavi et al. 2019; Van Den Oord et al. 2017] or video [Tan et al.
2021; Yu et al. 2022], naturally avoid such issues. They simplify the
training process, alleviating extensive hyperparameter tuning, while
maintaining high-quality representations. It reduces the complexity
of the action selection process by limiting the number of possible

actions, which simplifies exploration and optimization. Neural Cat-
egorical Prior (NCP) [Zhu et al. 2023] employs discrete latent space
into a hierarchical control pipeline for motion generation. We first
describe the policy architecture for the discrete latent model in NCP.

Our Part-wise Latent Tokens (PLT) extend the latent structure as
a set of discrete vectors to build highly adaptable motion prior for
a simulated character. NCP has not been extensively validated on
large-scale datasets or tested for generalization, leaving its broader
applicability unverified. As noted in previous works on advanced
quantization techniques [Mentzer et al. 2023; Yu et al. 2023], simply
increasing the codebook size does not guarantee NCP to improve the
fidelity of the latent representation. In contrast, our method explic-
itly combines part-wise latent tokens, which significantly improves
robustness and adaptability of the policies. We further modify the
training process in NCP to enhance scalability. Between the imita-
tion and task-learning stages, NCP introduces a prior-shifting stage,
in which a prior network predicts token indices based only on the
current state s. While this step tries to regularize the high-level
policy, we observe that it limits the performance when scaling to
large motion datasets. Our method eliminates this stage with only
the imitation and task-learning phases.

4.2 Refinement Network

In addition to the Part-wise Latent Tokens (PLT), we introduce
a refinement network to complement the discrete vector with a
continuous refinement vector Az and maintain whole-body consis-
tency. While PLT improves motion diversity and task performance,
simply combining part motions may lack global coordination. The
sampled motion sometimes introduces jerky artifacts or degrade
adaptability during balance-critical scenarios. The refinement net-
work outputs a continuous vector Az, given the concatenated latent
tokens (z1,Z2,:-- ,ZK) and the current state s. The refinement Az
is split into segments {Az,}x, each serving as an offset to its corre-
sponding latent token as 2, = zy, +Azx. Low-level policy mo, then
calculates action based on the refined token Z, and the current state
s. To allow only minimal modifications, we introduce a refinement
loss term:

This loss encourages the refinement network to predict subtle ad-
justments, ensuring smoother and more adaptable motions without
sacrificing part-wise flexibility. From the ablated studies, we find
this adjustment broadly helps combine part-wise latent tokens by
enhancing the physical stability. We illustrate the overall policy
architecture in Figure 3 (a) and (b).

4.3 Training

Imitation-Learning Phase. Rather using DRL, we utilize the online
distillation pipeline [Luo et al. 2023b] to train the imitation policy.
Conventional DRL algorithms often suffer from sample inefficiency
and instability due to the complexity of its optimization process,
which involves intricate loss terms. These challenges are further
exacerbated when training policies that rely on latent space regu-
larization, as additional terms are required to manage and structure
the latent space effectively. In contrast, online distillation offers a
more stable and efficient framework by simplifying the optimization
process. This simplification leads to better convergence and more
reliable policy as training outcomes. The process begins by training
an expert policy expert using PPO [Schulman et al. 2017]. Notably,

Task-Learning Phase. Our method maintains light-weight training
scheme for the high-level policy (Sec. 3). We use PPO [Schulman
et al. 2017] to train the discrete high-level policy mpjgp as described
in Sec. 3. Along with the codebooks and low-level polices, we fix
the parameters of refinement network during the task-learning
phase. Unlike to NCP [Zhu et al. 2023], our high-level policy uses
multi-discrete action space, each corresponding to the index of part-
wise latent token. For each task, we define the task-specific goal
observation g and goal-related rewards without extensive tuning.

Part-Wise Adaptation. We propose a scalable approach to adapt
motion priors in a continual and spatially localized manner, enabling
the combination of multiple datasets. Since our agent uses explicitly
decomposed priors for body parts, we can update only the motion
priors related to a specific body part. For instance, when modifying
the motion style of the first body segment (Figure 3 (c)), we update
only the parameters of the first codebook 8; and the corresponding
low-level policy 7jow,1. Additionally, we unfreeze the first layer of
the refinement network, which we find that it empirically stabilizes
the training process. We use PPO to perform this adaptation, as
training an expert policy for such a mixed motion setting is non-
trivial. This approach is particularly useful for animators who need
to refine motion styles for specific body parts without affecting
others. Furthermore, we observe that the agent retains a significant
portion of its previous motion priors after adaptation, ensuring both
flexibility and continuity in its motion capabilities.

5 Implementation

Dataset. We use three motion datasets for training and evaluation:
LaFAN1 [Harvey et al. 2020], AMASS [Mahmood et al. 2019], and the
Assassin dataset. LaFAN1 provides 2.9 hours of high-quality training
motions, including balance-critical actions like sprints, jumps, and
transitions. On the other hand, AMASS offers a larger dataset with
40 hours of training motions, prioritizing variety over dynamics but
predominantly featuring simpler motions like walking or running.
Following PHC [Luo et al. 2023a], we filter out invalid motions
(e.g., stair climbing) for physically simulated agents. The Assassin
dataset, a custom dataset from ActorCore [Reallusion 2025], contains

46 stylized sequences (5 minutes total) of assassin-like movements
(e.g., crouching, side-stepping) and is used for out-of-distribution
evaluation and part-wise adaptation experiments.

Baselines. To evaluate our model, we compare PLT with two
state-of-the-art baselines: PULSE [Luo et al. 2023b] and NCP [Zhu
et al. 2023], representing continuous and discrete latent models,
respectively. Due to differences in training pipelines and humanoid
models, direct comparisons with respective released codebases are
non-trivial. We address this by synchronizing all baselines in two
unified environments, one for LaFAN1 and Assassin, and another
for AMASS. For LaFAN1, we re-implement PULSE and NCP to
standardize the training setup, fine-tuning hyperparameters like
loss weights for fair comparison. For AMASS, we directly use the
original PULSE codebase and implement our PLT model alongside
it for evaluation under consistent criteria.

Training Details. We train agents using Isaac Gym [Makoviychuk
et al. 2021], a GPU-accelerated simulator supporting parallel train-
ing, with both low-level and high-level policies running at 30 Hz.
For LaFAN1, we use the AMP humanoid [Peng et al. 2021] with 28
degrees of freedom (DoF), and for AMASS, we use the SMPL-based
humanoid [Luo et al. 2023b] with 69 DoF. All reference motions are
retargeted to their respective humanoid models for consistency. Our
method supports three body partitioning modes: (1) PLT-1 (single,
non-partitioned group), (2) PLT-2 (upper and lower body groups),
and (3) PLT-5 (trunk, two arms, and two legs). We note NCP is equiv-
alent to PLT-1 without the refinement network. For fairness, we
allocate the same number of parameters for codebook tokens across
all configurations. We incorporate RVQ [Lee et al. 2022; Zeghidour
et al. 2021] as an advanced VQ layer for NCP and PLT. Consistent
with prior works [Guo et al. 2024; Yao et al. 2024], RVO improves
imitation quality over conventional VQ [Van Den Oord et al. 2017].
The high-level policy predicts token indices only in the first RVQ
codebook, as most information is concentrated in its first layer [Yang
et al. 2023]. Additional details, including RL hyperparameters and
codebook configurations, are provided in Appendix.

6.2 Task-Learning Results

N-Body Tracking. To evaluate the effectiveness during the task-
learning phase, we consider the N-body tracking problem, where an
agent tracks sparsely chosen trackers. Following prior works [Luo
et al. 2023b; Winkler et al. 2022], we generate synthetic tracker
trajectories sampled from the motion capture dataset. Unlike full-
body imitation rewards, our reward terms and early termination
conditions depend solely on the sparse trackers. This setup does
not require full-body reference motion when training the high-level
policy, such that we can evaluate the exploitation of motion prior
for the task execution. Using the imitation policy pretrained on
LaFAN1 dataset, we train high-level polices with LaFAN1 train set

and evaluate their tracking performance on unseen trajectories from
the test set. The evaluation uses the same full-body tracking met-
rics and number of trajectories as in Table 1. As shown in Table 3,
our PLT model significantly outperforms the baselines in both mo-
tion quality (tracking metrics, e.g. MPJPE) and tracking accuracy
(normalized return). As the number of trackers (N) increases, intro-
ducing more complex constraints, the use of more body groups and
the refinement network leads to notable improvements in the task
performance. Notably, as illustrated in Figure 4, our model with 5
body parts reliably tracks rapidly moving targets while maintaining
balance and naturalness. The N-body tracking results highlight the
effectiveness of our part-wise approach in achieving robust task
performance while faithfully preserving the motion patterns from
the original dataset.

Point-Goal Navigation. For the point-goal navigation task, we
train an agent to reach a specified goal on the ground as quickly as
possible. In the plain setup where the agent navigates flat terrain,
our partitioned body groups effectively combine part-wise skills
to enhance stability and speed, as shown in Figure 5 (a). To test
adaptability, we introduce a novel environment where the agent
must navigate with a randomly damaged body, namely damaged
point-goal navigation. During training, we randomly reduce the
torque limits in the joints on one side of an arm and leg by 90-100%,
creating 4096 distinct damage configurations across parallel environ-
ments. A unified policy is then trained to handle all configurations
simultaneously. In this challenging setup, the advantages of our
part-wise approach become more evident, as shown in Figure 5 (b).
Notably, the model with the refinement network (PLT-5) signifi-
cantly outperforms the model without it (PLT-5-noR), demonstrat-
ing improved physical stability and adaptability through enhanced
body coordination. Figure 5 (c) shows a single policy discovering
optimal movements for each damage configuration, often mirroring
real-world compensatory behaviors.

Compared to training from scratch on combined datasets, our
part-wise adaptation offers two key advantages. First, adaptation
requires only a few hours of training on an RTX 3090, as it updates
significantly fewer parameters. This efficiency allows for repeated
and continual refinement of motion priors. Second, the adapted
model retains reasonable fidelity to the original dataset, as shown in
Figure 7 (a). We attribute this to our efficient update scheme, where
non-updated part-wise latent tokens and low-level policies remain
unchanged. Also, we additionally observe that the refinement net-
work contributes to the better adaptation result.

We further evaluate the impact of body partitioning by comparing
models with two body parts (PLT-2) and five body parts (PLT-5), as
shown in Figure 7 (b). For PLT-5, we update the motion priors of
the legs during the lower-body adaptation phase, and the trunk and
arms during the upper-body adaptation phase, respectively. The
results indicate that increasing the granularity of body partitioning
enhances the adaptability and quality of the adapted motions.

7 Conclusion

This work introduces a novel policy architecture that leverages
part-wise latent tokens to enhance motion fidelity, adaptability, and
scalability in physically-based animation. By decomposing motion
priors into part-specific codebooks, our method dynamically com-
bines part-wise latent tokens to generate diverse and task-optimal
motions. This approach addresses limitations in whole-body motion
representations, enabling more efficient and generalizable solutions
compared to existing methods. The inclusion of a refinement net-
work further ensures coherence across body parts, mitigating issues
like unstable motions observed in naive part-wise combinations.
Through extensive experiments, we demonstrate the effective-
ness of our model across a wide range of scenarios. In the imitation-
learning phase, our PLT model outperforms state-of-the-art base-
lines in tracking accuracy and robustness, even when tested on

out-of-distribution targets. During task-learning, our approach ex-
hibits superior performance in challenging environments, including
N-body tracking and point-goal navigation with damaged body con-
figurations. Additionally, our method demonstrates scalability on
the large-scale AMASS dataset and excels in tasks like striking and
navigating rough terrain. We also highlight the flexibility of our part-
wise motion priors with a novel adaptation pipeline that supports
incremental updates to specific body parts without compromising
previously learned motions. This capability unlocks new possibili-
ties for combining motion styles, liberating physics-based animation
from the constraints of predefined full-body motion datasets.

Limitations. While our method provides a versatile framework for
complex motion tasks, it has certain limitations. First, our approach
relies on manually defined body decompositions. Although we show
that a five-part configuration generally outperforms a two-part
configuration, the optimal setup may vary depending on the task.
One may benefit from an automatic partitioning strategy to identify
the most effective configurations with more complex humanoid
models. Second, using separate codebooks and low-level policies for
each body part can create redundancies, such as duplicating skill
priors for both arms, which unnecessarily increases the parameter
count. We could address this by exploring shared codebook systems,
as suggested in prior work [Li et al. 2024]. For future applications,
we can further explore utilizing the part-wise latent token across
broader domains such as multi-agent scenarios or real-time control
applications.
